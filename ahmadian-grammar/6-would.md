## 1. The Grammar Basics

**Pronunciation:** *would* sounds like *wood* — the **l** is silent.

`would` is a **modal verb**.

**All modal verbs:** would – will – should – shall – can – could – may – might – must

### Rule #1: Modal verbs do not change their form

| Subject | Form |
| :--- | :--- |
| I | I **would** |
| You | You **would** |
| He / She / It | He / She / It **would** |
| We | We **would** |
| They | They **would** |

❌ *he woulds* · ❌ *I'm woulding* · ❌ *yesterday I woulded*

### Rule #2: modal + infinitive **without** `to`

`would + infinitive (without to)`

- ✅ *Kim **would go** to the beach every Saturday.*
- ❌ *Kim would **to** go to the beach every Saturday.*

### Rule #3: Negative = `would not` + infinitive

- ✅ *Kim **would not go** to the beach every Saturday.*
- ❌ *Kim don't would go to the beach every Saturday.*

### Short forms

| Full | Short |
| :--- | :--- |
| I would | **I'd** |
| You would | **You'd** |
| He would | **He'd** |
| She would | **She'd** |
| It would | **It'd** |
| We would | **We'd** |
| They would | **They'd** |

## 2. Would Like / Would You Mind

### Would like — a polite way to say "I want"

| Polite (would like) | Direct (want) |
| :--- | :--- |
| I'd like to see a movie tonight. | I want to see a movie tonight. |
| I'd like to buy a sailboat. | I want to buy a sailboat. |
| My flatmate would like to paint the kitchen green. | My flatmate wants to paint the kitchen green. |
| My parents would like to meet you. | My parents want to meet you. |

**Questions:**

| Polite | Direct |
| :--- | :--- |
| Would you like to drink tea or coffee? | Do you want to drink tea or coffee? |
| Would you like to go out tonight? | Do you want to go out tonight? |

⚠️ **Common mistake:** `would like` + **to + infinitive** — never `-ing`.

- ✅ *I **like drinking** tea with honey.* (general preference)
- ❌ *I would like **drinking** tea with honey.*
- ✅ *I would like **to drink** tea with honey.*

### Would you mind — a polite way to say "Can you please...?"

**Structure:** `Would you mind + verb-ing`

| Polite (would you mind) | Direct (can you please) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Would you mind **closing** the window? | Can you please close the window? |
| Would you mind **taking** the trash out tonight? | Can you please take the trash out tonight? |
| Would you mind **turning** down your music? | Can you please turn down your music? |

## 3. Hypothetical Situations in the Present / Future

**Hypothetical** = a situation that is **not the reality**.

**Structure:** `If + past simple, ... would + infinitive (without to)`

| Sentence | Reality |
| :--- | :--- |
| If I had a lot of money, I **would go** on holiday. | Do I have a lot of money? **No.** Can I go on holiday? **No.** |
| If I lived in Japan, I **would eat** sushi every day. | Do I live in Japan? **No.** Can I eat sushi every day? **No.** |
| If I didn't have a car, I **would cycle** to work every day. | I **do** have a car, so I don't need to cycle. |

### Giving advice: "If I were you, I would..."

**Advice** = something we say to help someone.

It's very typical to give advice with **"If I were you, I would..."**

> **A:** My neck has been killing me lately.
>
> **B:** If I were you, I **would see** a doctor.

> **A:** My landlord wants to increase my rent.
>
> **B:** If I were you, I **would find** another flat. Yours is already so expensive.

> **A:** My ex-boyfriend invited me to his wedding.
>
> **B:** If I were you, I **wouldn't go**.

### Important rule: "I **were**", not "I was"

| Situation | Form |
| :--- | :--- |
| Normal | I **was** |
| Hypothetical | I **were** ✅ |

- ✅ *If **I were** you, I would...*

## 4. Hypothetical Situations in the Past

**Structure:** `would have + past participle (V₃)`

| Sentence | Reality |
| :--- | :--- |
| If I had studied more when I was younger, I **would have been** a doctor. | Did I study? **No.** Did I become a doctor? **No.** |
| If I had left my house two minutes earlier, I **wouldn't have missed** my train. | Did I leave on time? **No.** Did I catch my train? **No.** |
| If I had known you were coming, I **would have made** dinner. | Did I know you were coming? **No.** Did I make dinner? **No.** |

## 5. Giving Polite Excuses

**Structure:** `would (love to), but + reason`

**Excuse** = something we say to tell someone **why** we can't do something.

> **A:** Are you coming to my party tomorrow?
>
> **B:** I **would love to, but** tomorrow I am going to London.

*Why can't I go to the party? → I'm going to London. (that's my excuse)*

> **A:** I need someone to drive me to the airport tomorrow. Are you free?
>
> **B:** I **would, but** tomorrow I'm working.

*Why can't I drive my friend to the airport? → Because I'm working. (that's my excuse)*

💡 **"love to" is optional** — you don't need to say it.

---

## 6. Past Habits

**Structure:** `would + infinitive` — something that was normal in the past (but not anymore).

- When I was a teenager, I **would wake up** late every day. *(now I wake up very early)*
- My mom **would pack** my lunch for me every day when I went to school.
- In England, people **would drink** more tea than they do now.

### ⚠️ Stative verbs: don't use `would`

You **cannot** use `would + infinitive` with **stative verbs** (emotional / non-action verbs).

**Stative verbs:** know, remember, believe, love, ...

- ❌ *I **would love** the Spice Girls.*
- ✅ *I **used to love** the Spice Girls.*

**Why?** Because *love* is a stative verb, and here I'm talking about a past habit.

> Don't confuse this with "I'd love to..." from sections 2 and 5 — that's the polite *want*, not a past habit.

## 🔍 Quick Summary

| # | Use | Pattern | Example |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Polite "I want" | `would like to + infinitive` | I'd like to see a movie tonight. |
| 2 | Polite request | `would you mind + verb-ing` | Would you mind closing the window? |
| 3 | Hypothetical (present/future) | `If + past simple, would + infinitive` | If I had money, I would go on holiday. |
| 4 | Advice | `If I were you, I would + infinitive` | If I were you, I would see a doctor. |
| 5 | Hypothetical (past) | `would have + V₃` | I would have made dinner. |
| 6 | Polite excuse | `would (love to), but + reason` | I would, but tomorrow I'm working. |
| 7 | Past habit | `would + infinitive` (action verbs only) | I would wake up late every day. |
