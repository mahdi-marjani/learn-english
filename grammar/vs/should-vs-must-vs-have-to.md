# 📘 Should vs Must vs Have To (Cheat Sheet)

In order of strength: **should** (advice) → **have to** (outside obligation) → **must** (strong obligation or certainty).

---

## 1. SHOULD
**Meaning:** Advice or recommendation — not obligatory.
**Examples:**
- You **should** see a doctor.
- We **should** leave soon.

## 2. MUST
**Meaning:** Strong obligation (often the speaker's own feeling) or logical certainty. No past form — use *had to*.
**Examples:**
- I **must** finish this today.
- She **must** be tired.

## 3. HAVE TO
**Meaning:** Obligation from an outside source (rules, laws, other people).
**Examples:**
- I **have to** wear a uniform at work.
- She **had to** cancel the trip.

⚠️ **mustn't** = forbidden. **don't have to** = optional. Opposites, not synonyms.
- You **mustn't** park here. *(not allowed)*
- You **don't have to** park here. *(allowed, but not required)*

---

## 🔍 Comparison

| Feature | SHOULD | MUST | HAVE TO |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Strength | Weak (advice) | Strong | Strong |
| Source | Speaker's opinion | Speaker's feeling / rule | Outside authority |
| Past form | should have + V₃ | had to | had to |
