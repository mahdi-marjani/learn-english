# 📘 Countable vs Uncountable (Cheat Sheet)

**Countable** nouns can be counted one by one (*an apple, two apples*). **Uncountable** nouns are a mass or an idea you can't count directly (*water, advice*). The difference decides which **article**, **verb** and **quantifier** you can use.

**Quick test:** can you put a number right before it? *two books* ✅ → countable. *two waters / two advices* ❌ → uncountable (or needs a unit word).

---

## 1. COUNTABLE
**Meaning:** Things you can count — they have a singular **and** a plural form.
**Examples:**
- a **book** → two **books**
- one **student** → many **students**

**Goes with:** a/an, numbers, **many**, **few / a few**, **fewer**, **several**, **how many**.
**Verb:** singular noun → singular verb, plural noun → plural verb.

## 2. UNCOUNTABLE
**Meaning:** Substances, liquids, abstract ideas and groups — no plural form, no *a/an*, no direct numbers.
**Examples:**
- I need some **water**.
- The **news** is good.

**Goes with:** **much**, **little / a little**, **less**, **how much**.
**Verb:** always **singular**.

To count them, add a **unit word**: *a glass of water, a piece of advice, a slice of bread, a cup of tea.*

---

## 3. Common Traps
These are uncountable in English, even though you might expect them to be countable:

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Correct |
| :--- | :--- |
| an advice / advices | some advice / **a piece of** advice |
| informations | some information / **a piece of** information |
| furnitures | some furniture / **a piece of** furniture |
| many homeworks | a lot of homework |
| The news **are** bad. | The news **is** bad. |
| The traffic **are** terrible. | The traffic **is** terrible. |

⚠️ Other common uncountables: *money, luggage, equipment, weather, progress, music, rice, bread.*

---

## 4. Nouns That Can Be Both
Same word, but the meaning changes:

| Noun | Uncountable | Countable |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **coffee** | I like coffee. *(the drink)* | I'll have a coffee. *(a cup of it)* |
| **time** | I don't have much time. | I've seen it three times. *(occasions)* |
| **paper** | I need some paper. *(material)* | She wrote a paper. *(an essay)* |
| **hair** | She has long hair. | There's a hair in my soup. *(one strand)* |
| **experience** | I have experience in Python. | It was a great experience. *(an event)* |

---

## 5. Quantifiers

| Quantifier | Countable | Uncountable |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **many** | ✅ many books | ❌ |
| **much** | ❌ | ✅ much time |
| **a lot of / lots of** | ✅ | ✅ |
| **some / any / no** | ✅ | ✅ |
| **enough / plenty of** | ✅ | ✅ |
| **few / a few / fewer** | ✅ a few books | ❌ |
| **little / a little / less** | ❌ | ✅ a little time |
| **several** | ✅ | ❌ |
| **how many / how much** | how **many** books | how **much** time |

⚠️ **few / little** (no "a") = *almost none* → negative feeling. **a few / a little** = *some* → positive.
- I have **a few** friends. *(some — good)*
- I have **few** friends. *(almost none — sad)*

⚠️ **much** sounds unnatural in plain positive sentences → use **a lot of**.
- ❌ I have much money. → ✅ I have **a lot of** money.
- *too much, so much, very much, as much* are fine.

---

## 6. Not Many / Not Much
**Pattern:**

`Not many` + *plural countable noun* + **positive** verb
`Not much` + *uncountable noun* + **singular** verb

**Examples:**
- **Not many** people **have seen** this movie. *(≈ Few people have seen it.)*
- **Not many** students passed the exam.
- **Not much** time **is** left. *(≈ Little time is left.)*

⚠️ The **not** negates the **amount**, not the verb — so the verb stays **positive** (*have seen*, not *haven't seen*). Compare:
- **Not many** people have seen it. → the number who **have** seen it is small.
- **Many** people **haven't** seen it. → the number who **haven't** seen it is large.

**Same idea — partial negation:** *not all, not every, not everyone*
- **Not everyone** likes this movie. *(some do, some don't)*

## 🔍 Comparison

| Feature | COUNTABLE | UNCOUNTABLE |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Plural form | ✅ books | ❌ ~~waters~~ |
| a / an | ✅ a book | ❌ ~~an advice~~ |
| Direct numbers | ✅ two books | ❌ needs a unit: two **glasses of** water |
| "how ...?" | how **many** | how **much** |
| Big amount | many | much (or a lot of) |
| Small amount | few / a few / fewer | little / a little / less |
| Negated amount | not **many** | not **much** |
| Verb | singular or plural | always singular |
| Examples | book, student, idea | water, advice, news |
