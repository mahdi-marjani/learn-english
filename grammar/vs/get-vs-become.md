## GET vs BECOME (Cheat Sheet)

Both words are used in the sense of **"begin to be,"** so many non-native speakers mix them up — Luckily, there's a simple rule.

---

### 1. GET
- **Meaning:** A **progressive state** — used with an **adjective**, for when things **are changing**.
- **Common use:** More common in recent spoken English. Often used with a comparative (words with **-er**, or **more** ___).
- **Note:** Get has some other meanings too, but this cheat sheet focuses on this one.

**Examples:**
- The weather's **getting** colder.
- I want to **get** better at speaking in English.
- The stock prices are **getting** even higher.

---

### 2. BECOME
- **Meaning:** A **complete change** — used with a **noun**. The difference seems to be very large.

**Examples:**
- The student **became** the teacher.
- New York has **become** a safer city in the last twenty years.
- The audience **became** so silent, you could hear a pin drop.

---

### VS (With Adjectives)

Both **get** and **become** can be used with adjectives:
- He **got** angry.
- He **became** angry.

**Got** seems more **progressive**; **became** seems more **complete**. Where do you draw the line? That's up to you.

| **Feature** | **GET** | **BECOME** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Goes with** | Adjective | Noun |
| **Type of change** | Progressive (things are changing) | Complete (a large difference) |
| **Spoken English** | Far more common | Less common |
