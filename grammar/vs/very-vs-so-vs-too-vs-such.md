# 📘 Very vs So vs Too vs Such (Cheat Sheet)

Four intensifiers, four jobs: **very** just intensifies, **so** intensifies + gives a result, **too** means "more than needed" (a problem), **such** does the job of *so* but in front of a noun.

---

## 1. VERY
**Meaning:** A high degree — plain intensifier, no result implied.
**Examples:**
- The soup is **very** hot.
- She runs **very** fast.

## 2. SO
**Meaning:** A high degree, often with a result (using *that*). Goes before an adjective/adverb — no noun.
**Examples:**
- The soup is **so** hot (that I can't eat it).
- He speaks **so** quickly.

## 3. TOO
**Meaning:** More than wanted/needed — usually a problem. Often followed by *to* + verb.
**Examples:**
- The soup is **too** hot (to eat).
- She's **too** young to drive.

## 4. SUCH
**Meaning:** Same job as *so*, but placed before **(a/an) + (adjective) + noun**.
**Examples:**
- It's **such** a hot day (that we stayed inside).
- They are **such** nice people.

⚠️ ❌ *It's so hot soup.* → ✅ It's **such** hot soup. *(a noun needs "such," not "so")*

---

## 🔍 Comparison

| Feature | VERY | SO | TOO | SUCH |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Followed by | adjective/adverb | adjective/adverb | adjective/adverb | noun (+ adjective) |
| Implies | plain intensity | intensity + result | excess/problem | intensity + result |
| Pattern | very + adj | so + adj (+ that) | too + adj (+ to) | such + a + adj + noun |
