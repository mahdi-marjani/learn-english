## 2. Basics

### Infinitive, bare infinitive, gerund

| Infinitive / "to" infinitive (`to` + base verb) | Bare infinitive / zero infinitive | Gerund (base verb + `ing`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| to eat | eat | eating |
| to play | play | playing |
| to go | go | going |
| to find | find | finding |
| to pay | pay | paying |
| to be | be | being |
| to study | study | studying |
| to try | try | trying |
| to have | have | having |

### A gerund is not a verb

Infinitives are verbs, but gerunds are **not** verbs.

Examples that look like gerunds but aren't:

- I **am talking** to you → `present continuous`
- They'**ve been studying** really hard → `present perfect continuous`

These are **not** gerunds.

A gerund is also **not an adjective**:

- I read an **interesting** book → `adjective`

**GERUND:** an "ing" word that looks like a verb but functions as a noun.

### Gerunds vs infinitives

| | Gerunds | Infinitives |
| :--- | :-: | :-: |
| subject | ✅ | ✅ |
| showing purpose | ❌ | ✅ |
| giving instructions | ❌ | ✅ |

## 3. Gerunds

If you want your verb to be a **subject**, it must be a gerund.

| Sentence | Don't say | The subject is |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Clean**ing** is hard work | ❌ `To clean...` | `Cleaning` |
| Walk**ing** to work every day has really helped my back problem | ❌ `To walk...` | `Walking to work every day` (noun phrase) |
| Driv**ing** without a seatbelt is dangerous | ❌ `To drive...` | `Driving without a seatbelt` (noun phrase) |

- ❌ `To smoke` has many negative effects on your health
- ✅ `Smoking` has many negative effects on your health

## 4. Infinitive Subjects

Infinitive subjects are often very `abstract` and `poetic`:

> Shakespeare: To be, or not to be, that is the question (old English)

If we use an infinitive subject, we're usually talking about `goals` and `achievements`.

A: Did you hear? Samantha quit medical school

| Infinitive subject | Gerund version |
| :--- | :--- |
| B: `To study` for so long just to quit **seems** like a waste | ✅ `Studying...` |
| `To become` a doctor had always **been** her dream | ✅ `Becoming...` |
| `To pass` my econometrics class this semester **is** my only goal | ✅ `Passing...` |

## 5. In order to

These sentences start with an infinitive. A gerund is not possible here:

| Short form | Not this | Complete version |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `To vote`, you must be 18 | ❌ `Voting...` | `In order to` **vote**, you must be 18 |
| `To win` the race, you will need to start training every day | ❌ `Winning...` | `In order to` **win** the race, you will need to start training every day |
| `To make` employees feel safer, the company installed security cameras | ❌ `Making...` | `In order to` **make** employees feel safer, the company installed security cameras |

These are the short form of `In order to`.

### Purpose

IN ORDER TO... : `show purpose` · `why we do something`

| Action | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- |
| I wear glasses, | (in order) to see |

### Switching the clauses

The clauses can be switched around:

- You must be 18 (in order) **to vote**
- You will need to start training every day (in order) **to win** the race
- The company installed security cameras (in order) **to make** employees feel safer

### To vs In order to

In spoken English, `in order to` isn't really common.

### Comma

If the *to* phrase comes **first**, we need a `comma`:

- **To vote**`,` you must be 18

If the *to* phrase comes **later**, we don't need a `comma`:

- You must be 18 **to vote**

### Instructions

- **To listen** to the message again, press 1
- **To save** the message, press 2
- **To delete** the message, press 3

## 6. Grammar Lists

### List 1: Verbs + gerund

These verbs are **always** followed by the gerund:

admit, anticipate, avoid, can't help, consider, deny, feel like, involve, mention, practice, recommend, resume, risk, suggest, understand

| Verb | Definition | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `avoid` | to stay away from someone or something | I `avoid` sitt**ing** in the sun<br>You should `avoid` star**ing** at a screen for too long |
| `involve` | requires / this is necessary | Learning a language `involves` study**ing** a little bit every day |

### List 2: Verbs + infinitive

These verbs are **always** followed by the infinitive:

agree, ask, decide, deserve, expect, hope, learn, manage, need, threaten, wait, want, volunteer, offer, plan, promise, refuse, seem, struggle

| Verb | Definition | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `manage` | to successfully complete a difficult task | I finally `managed` **to find** a babysitter for Friday night |
| `refuse` | to say 'no' when somebody asks you to do or have something | My son `refuses` **to eat** vegetables |

### List 3: Verbs + object + infinitive

ask, expect, hire, invite, order, remind, require, teach, tell, urge, warn

| Verb | Definition | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `ask` | it also exists in list 2 | I `asked` **to see** the menu (list 2)<br>I `asked` my friend **to help** me (list 3) |
| `remind` | to help someone remember | Please `remind` me **to call** mom later and say happy birthday |

### List 4: Verbs + gerund / infinitive (no change in meaning)

like, love, hate, prefer, begin, can't bear, can't stand, continue, propose, start

| Verb | Definition | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `can't stand` | to hate | ✅ I `can't stand` **seeing** you sad<br>✅ I `can't stand` **to see** you sad |
| `start` | — | ✅ The two drivers `started` **shouting** at each other<br>✅ The two drivers `started` **to shout** at each other |

### List 5: Verbs + gerund / infinitive (big change in meaning)

remember, forget, try, stop, regret

#### remember

| | + gerund | + infinitive |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **definition** | to have or keep an image in your memory of an event, a person, a place, etc. from the past | to bring back to your mind a fact, piece of information, etc. that you knew |
| **examples** | I `remember` learn**ing** how to swim with arm floaties<br><br>A: Did you see anything suspicious last night around 10 pm?<br>B: I `remember` see**ing** a man carrying a large suitcase | Please `remember` **to cancel** your dental appointment (Don't forget)<br><br>All students must `remember` **to bring** their passports with them (Don't forget)<br><br>I'm so happy you `remembered` **to turn off** the stove |

#### forget

(just the opposite of `remember`)

| | + gerund | + infinitive |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **definition** | I don't have the memory of a past experience / action | Oops, I didn't remember to do this task |
| **examples** | A: Look how cute you were when you were little<br>B: I will never `forget` play**ing** with Addie | Why do you always `forget` **to take out** the trash?<br><br>Oh man, I `forgot` **to get** gas |

#### try

| | + gerund | + infinitive |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **definition** | You do different things to find the best solution. You experiment | **present/future:** I will attempt this action, but I think it will be difficult<br><br>**past:** I tried, but it wasn't successful |
| **examples** | A: I can't sleep at night<br>B: Have you `tried` meditat**ing**?<br>A: Yes<br>B: Have you `tried` drink**ing** herbal tea?<br>A: Yes<br>B: Have you `tried` listen**ing** to classical music?<br>A: Yes Yes Yes Yes!<br>B: You're so cranky. You should `try` sleep**ing** more | A: Everyone can do yoga. It's easy<br>B: I can `try` **to do** that<br>A: Next<br>B: I can `try` **to do** that one<br>A: Next<br>B: I think she's `trying` **to kill** me<br><br>I `tried` **to get** tickets for the 7 o'clock show, but they were sold out |

#### stop

| | + gerund | + infinitive |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **definition** | Don't do this anymore | I stopped one action because I wanted to do another action |
| **examples** | Can you please `stop` chew**ing** your gum so loudly?<br><br>I `stopped` go**ing** to my French class | I ran almost a full hour. But, I did `stop` **to take** pictures of some ducklings<br><br>The cars `stopped` **to let** the ambulance pass |

#### regret

| | + gerund | + infinitive |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **definition** | Why did I do this in the past? I feel guilty | (very formal) I'm sorry. I need to give you bad news |
| **examples** | I `regret` not spend**ing** more time with my grandma when she was alive<br><br>I `regret` eat**ing** all of those donuts | We `regret` **to inform** you that your application has been rejected<br><br>I `regret` **to announce** Fiona's decision to leave our company |
