### 1. Simple Present

**🏗️ Structure**

`Subject + get/gets + past participle (V₃) + ...`

**✅ Examples**

- **He gets paid every Friday.**
- **The mail gets delivered around noon.**
- **These machines get checked once a month.**

---

### 2. Simple Past

**🏗️ Structure**

`Subject + got + past participle + ...`

**✅ Examples**

- **The window got broken last night.**
- **My phone got stolen at the airport.**
- **We got invited to the wedding.**

---

### 3. Present Perfect

**🏗️ Structure**

`Subject + have/has + got/gotten + past participle + ...`

📌 American English usually uses **gotten**; British English usually uses **got**.

**✅ Examples**

- **She has gotten promoted this year.** *(American English)*
- **She has got promoted this year.** *(British English)*
- **They have gotten married twice.**
- **The report has gotten finished ahead of schedule.**

---

### 4. Modal Verbs

**🏗️ Structure**

`modal + get + past participle + ...`

**✅ Examples**

- **You might get fired if you're late again.**
- **This could get finished by tomorrow.**
- **You should get checked by a doctor.**
- **The car must get fixed before the trip.**

---

### 5. Future Simple

**🏗️ Structure**

`Subject + will + get + past participle + ...`

**✅ Examples**

- **I hope the car will get fixed before the trip.**
- **The house will get painted next week.**
- **You'll get paid as soon as the project ends.**

📌 In this tense, `will be fixed` is more natural and common than `will get fixed`; the get-form sounds a bit more informal.

---

## ⚠️ Important Limitations

Unlike be-passive, which is used across all 8 tenses, get-passive has the following limitations:

- **Continuous and perfect continuous forms are almost never used:**
  For example, `is getting being cleaned` or `has been getting cleaned` aren't natural, and no one really uses them.

- **Not formal:** get-passive is mostly used in speech and informal writing; use be-passive in essays, reports, or formal writing.
  - Informal: **The budget got approved yesterday.**
  - Formal: **The budget was approved yesterday.**

- **Rarely appears with "by + agent":**
  - ✅ *The cake was made by my mom.*
  - ❌ *The cake got made by my mom.* (unnatural)
  - ✅ *The window was broken by the storm.*
  - ❌ *The window got broken by the storm.* (unnatural)

- **Doesn't work with verbs that have no passive form at all:** verbs like *happen, sleep, seem, become* can't be made passive with get, just as they can't with be.
  - ❌ *The accident got happened.*
  - ❌ *She got seemed tired.*

## 🔍 Get-Passive vs Be-Passive

| Feature | Be-Passive | Get-Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Formality | Formal / neutral | Informal / conversational |
| Common use | Writing, news, formal texts | Everyday conversation |
| Agent (by...) | Natural and common | Rare and often awkward |
| Meaning focus | Describes a state | Emphasizes change or an event (often sudden) |
| Usable tenses | All 8 tenses (though some are rare) | Mainly simple present, simple past, present perfect, and modals |

**✅ Examples (same event, two styles)**

- **The thief was caught by the police.** *(be-passive, neutral/formal, agent included)*
- **The thief got caught.** *(get-passive, informal, agent dropped)*
- **The documents were signed yesterday.** *(be-passive)*
- **The documents got signed yesterday.** *(get-passive)*

---

## 💡 Quick Tips

- **get-passive** is typically used for sudden, unexpected, or personal events:
  *get married, get lost, get fired, get hurt, get promoted, get invited, get dressed*

  **✅ Examples**
  - **They got married last summer.**
  - **I got lost on my way home.**
  - **He got hurt during the game.**

- Watch out for a similar-looking structure that is **not** passive: `get + adjective`, not `get + past participle`:
  *get angry, get tired, get hungry, get bored*

  **✅ Examples**
  - **She gets angry very easily.**
  - **I got tired after the long walk.**
  - **The kids got bored during the lecture.**

  Here, angry/tired/hungry/bored are adjectives, not the third form of the verb (V₃).

- To spot a real passive, check whether the word after get is a past participle (V₃) rather than a plain adjective.

- In formal or academic writing, always prefer **be-passive**; get-passive is for conversation.
